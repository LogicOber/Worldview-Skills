# Thriller Plot

Write a story where the audience knows something the hero does not, and cannot look away.

## What the user gets

| Deliverable | Format |
|---|---|
| Spine | want · obstacle · stakes · dilemma · change — per `film-story` |
| Clock | the deadline, how the audience sees it, how it tightens at each act break |
| Antagonist plan | what they want, their steps, which steps the audience sees before the hero does |
| Reversal ladder | 4–7 reversals, each removing an option the hero had |
| Midpoint | the moment the hero's goal changes (from survive → stop them; from prove → protect) |
| Climax | the collision of plan and counter-plan; the dilemma with two costly horns |
| Emotion map | per block; dread is the primary register, relief is rationed |
| Script hand-off | to `film-script`, `horror-chase`, `narrative-choice` |

## Rules this entry enforces

- **A visible clock.** A countdown, a date, a rising tide. The audience must be able to feel time passing without being told.
- **Dramatic irony is engineered.** At least two scenes where the audience knows the danger and the hero does not.
- **Reversals remove options.** Each one closes a door the hero was going to use. By the climax, only the costly door remains.
- **The antagonist is competent.** Their plan would work. The hero wins by understanding it, not by the villain's mistake.
- **The midpoint changes the want.** Not the plan — the want.
- **Relief is rationed.** One breath per act, then tighter.
- **Loss lands before the climax.** Someone or something the hero needed is gone going in.

## Example

```text
Clock: the gate seals permanently at dawn; the sky lightens a little every block
Antagonist plan: the Guardian is drawing the city's light into the core to
    wake fully; the audience sees lanterns dying from the Overlook before
     the party understands why
Reversals: the bridge falls (no direct route) · the lantern keeper's wick is
   the last light (no reserve) · Ines admits she closed the door (no
clean hero) · the spirit must burn itself (no guide)
Midpoint: from "open the gate" to "decide what Corin becomes"
Climax: destroy the core (Corin freed, city dies) vs preserve (Corin stays
     as light, never a person)
```
