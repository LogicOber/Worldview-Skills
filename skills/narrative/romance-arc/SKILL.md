# Romance Arc

Write two people who are each other's obstacle and each other's answer.

## What the user gets

| Deliverable | Format |
|---|---|
| Two spines | want · obstacle · stakes for each lead — they must conflict |
| The orbit | the reason they cannot simply leave (work, family, quest, a shared debt) |
| Intimacy ladder | 6–10 beats of rising closeness, each tested |
| The wound | the lie, the omission, the old hurt that will detonate |
| Separation | the beat where both are right and both are alone |
| Reunion as choice | what each gives up to return; what is different |
| Emotion map | longing is the register; comedy is allowed only with sadness under it |
| Script hand-off | to `film-script`, `visual-novel`, `narrative-choice` |

## Rules this entry enforces

- **Two spines, not one.** Both leads want something concrete and their wants collide.
- **The orbit is external.** They stay near each other for a reason that is not yet love.
- **Each intimacy beat is tested.** A step closer is followed by a reason to step back.
- **The wound is planted early.** The audience sees the omission before the other lead does.
- **Separation is fair.** Neither is the villain of the break.
- **Reunion costs.** Someone gives up a thing they wanted at the start. If nothing is given up, it is a reward scene, not a romance.
- **Show, do not narrate.** Per `film-story`: feeling is carried by a face and an object, not by voice-over.

## Example

```text
A: Ana wants to keep the world hers — finished or not, hers.
B: Tom wants to know what happens next — and to be part of it.
Orbit: he is the first player who understood her city; she needs a player
     who understands to finish it
Ladder: he leaves a note · she reads it twice · she invites collaboration ·
      they co-edit a card · he says "we didn't want you to finish it for us" ·
      she opens the box she taped shut
Wound: she never told anyone she does not know the ending
Separation: he asks her to decide Corin's fate; she cannot; he leaves the board
Reunion: she puts a blank card on the board and gives him edit rights —
     she gives up authorship; he gives up the answer
```
