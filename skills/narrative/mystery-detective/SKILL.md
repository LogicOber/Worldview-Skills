# Mystery / Detective

Write a puzzle the reader could solve, and most will not.

## What the user gets

| Deliverable | Format |
|---|---|
| The case | what happened, where, when; what is known at the start |
| Suspect grid | 3–6 suspects: motive · means · opportunity · the lie each tells · the truth behind it |
| Clue trail | every clue, where it is found, what it seems to mean, what it actually means |
| Misdirection | one honest red herring — true facts that point the wrong way |
| Detective's arc | what the investigator wants beyond the answer; what solving costs them |
| Reveal | the scene, and the earlier scene it rewrites |
| Fair-play audit | can a reader solve it from the text alone? which clue is the key? |
| Script hand-off | to `film-script`, `visual-novel`, `narrative-choice` (interrogation trees) |

## Rules this entry enforces

- **Fair play.** Every clue needed to solve the case is on the page before the reveal. No information the reader could not have.
- **Every suspect lies.** About something; not necessarily the crime. The lies are the texture.
- **The red herring is true.** It misleads by arrangement, not by falsehood.
- **The reveal rewrites a scene.** A moment the reader saw one way now reads another. This is the payoff.
- **The detective pays.** Solving the case costs a relationship, a belief, a version of themselves.
- **Clue economy.** Each clue appears once as noise and once as signal. Nothing is stated three times.
- **Loss is present.** Per `film-story`: the victim mattered to someone we met.

## Example

```text
Case: three winters ago, the gatekeeper Corin vanished behind the Skull Gate.
      The city says he was a hero. The gate says UNFINISHED.
Suspects: Ines (his sister; lies about the door) · the lantern keeper (lies
   about the last light) · the blacksmith (lies about the shield) · the
     market child's mother (lies about the whistle)
Clues: the compass points to the gate, not north · three-short-two-long knock
       · the whistle Corin carried, now the child's · the lantern keeper's wick
   is the last of the old light
Red herring: the blacksmith's broken shield — true, but from a different night
Reveal: Ines closed the door herself. The earlier scene — "It only points
 somewhere I don't want to go" — was a confession, not a riddle.
Cost: Ines loses the city's version of her; the detective (the players) lose
     the clean hero they came for.
```
