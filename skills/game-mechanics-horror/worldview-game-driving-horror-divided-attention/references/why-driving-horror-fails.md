# Why driving horror and divided attention fail

## A cabin scare cannot borrow safety from the road

If the player must turn away during a blind bend, a crash is authored rather than earned. Build the road-demand curve first. Place long inspections on stable straights or at legal stops; reserve short mirror checks for moderate demand. An ignored optional check needs a later consequence that is tense but survivable.

## Looking away needs a budget the player can feel

A hidden timer makes failure arbitrary. Communicate decreasing margin through lane drift, road-edge motion, instrument cadence, or a staged warning. Return the camera forward before the last recoverable moment if an accessibility option requests it, but do not select the correct cabin target for the player.

## The threat cannot move whenever the camera turns

Camera-driven teleportation teaches players to flick the view rather than reason about space. Give the threat a last valid zone, a transition condition, and a travel or cover rule. A glance may reveal a transition; it does not cause one unless the fiction explicitly makes observation causal and teaches that rule.

## Stopping must alter more than speed

If stopping is always safe, every cabin event becomes parking. If it is always fatal, the stop control is a trap. A legal stop can improve inspection or repair while spending schedule, power, shelter, or distance from a known threat. An unsafe stop must be signaled by road geometry, traffic rule, terrain, or threat access.

## Common-sense driving responses should work or fail visibly

Players will brake, steer away, use lights, sound a warning, pull over, or attempt restart. Support those actions, or show why a specific action cannot work before it causes irreversible loss. Horror grows when a sensible response reveals a new limitation, not when the game pretends the response does not exist.

## Physical feedback and hostile feedback need distinct evidence

A jolt could mean a pothole, collision, cargo shift, or intrusion. Early encounters should establish distinct cue combinations. Later ambiguity can mix them, but the player should have learned what to inspect. Otherwise every sound becomes generic noise.

## More motion is not more fear

Excessive shake, blur, narrow field of view, and forced camera movement create discomfort before tension. Motion reduction must preserve route information. Horror should survive with shake disabled because the decision conflict exists in state and timing, not nausea.

## A vehicle failure needs a route forward

Stalling without a repair, shelter, signal, walkable fallback, or controlled restart produces dead time. Define the failure ceiling for every segment. A catastrophic failure may change how the player reaches the fixed ending, but it must not create an accidental second campaign or an unwinnable save.

## Save boundaries must include the cabin

Saving only road progress can resurrect cargo, duplicate a passenger, reset an interference, or move the threat. Commit road, vehicle, cabin, and threat state together. Restore the camera and controls only after the complete state is ready.

## Neighbor boundary

This Skill owns the moving vehicle and its attention conflict. It does not own exterior chase geometry, campaign-wide dependency loss, general sensory unreliability, or the ending system. Handoff fields must be explicit so those neighbors cannot rewrite vehicle truth.
