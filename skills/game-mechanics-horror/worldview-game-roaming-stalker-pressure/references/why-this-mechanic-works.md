# Why roaming stalker pressure works

## Presence can matter before contact

A persistent stalker changes how the player interprets empty space. A moved door, distant crossing, trace, interrupted signal, or known connector can alter a route before an attack begins. The player plans around possible presence because the threat has continuity and because its signs have learned meaning.

This is different from constant pursuit. Continuous contact consumes attention until every objective becomes the same escape task. Roaming pressure alternates evidence, approach, decision, consequence, and recovery. Absence is not inactivity; it is where anticipation and route planning happen.

Presence becomes actionable when traces form a territory grammar. Residue on only compatible connectors, a distant safe crossing, a ward that visibly changes access, or an approach sound tied to the route lets the player predict where contact can occur. Scratches everywhere and a global sting produce mood but teach no boundary.

## Spatial continuity makes uncertainty strategic

The player rarely needs exact coordinates. They need bounded inference: the stalker was in the east wing, the lift is locked, the maintenance bridge connects north, and a fresh trace appeared near the generator. Those facts narrow possible movement without guaranteeing safety.

Arbitrary spawning destroys that reasoning. If the stalker can appear in any unobserved point, learning the map has little value and checking a room offers no temporary knowledge. Preserving physical or declared abstract travel lets uncertainty coexist with agency.

Recent-visibility memory matters for the same reason. A player who cleared a dead end should be allowed to rely on that observation briefly. Spawning there the instant the camera turns does not create intelligence; it invalidates observation.

## Identity turns encounters into a relationship

One stable stalker can accumulate history: it was warded from a corridor, lost the player at a flooded stair, gained a new connector after power returned, or remains damaged after a costly defense. Persistence makes those events part of one relationship rather than disconnected set pieces.

Streaming and save/load are therefore mechanical concerns. Duplicating the actor or forgetting its altered state breaks more than technical continuity. It tells the player their previous decisions did not describe the same world.

## The director and the stalker need different authority

The director shapes rhythm. It can notice that calm has lasted, an objective is exposed, or a new encounter form is available. The stalker owns location, knowledge, movement, and attack permission.

Separating them prevents a pacing desire from becoming omniscience. The director can select an investigation opportunity near a world signal, but the stalker learns only what that signal reveals. The director can stage a legal route, but cannot place the stalker in a closed room it could not reach.

This boundary also improves debugging. A failed encounter can be traced to eligibility, candidate selection, travel, perception, counterplay, or combat instead of one opaque “AI intensity” value.

## Hard exclusions make variability trustworthy

Some constraints should not be probabilities. Spawning in view, inside collision, on a ladder exit, within a safe room, behind a door the player just checked, or where no counterplay exists is unfair even if it happens rarely.

Rejecting candidates protects the contract. A director that sometimes finds no legal encounter is behaving correctly. Non-contact evidence can maintain atmosphere while the world lacks a fair confrontation site.

Once constraints pass, seeded choice can produce variety among legal routes, warnings, and encounter forms. Reproducible seeds and history allow failures to be investigated without removing variation from ordinary play.

## Warning converts arrival into a decision

Foreshadowing supplies time and causal direction. The player hears a connector, sees drifting ash, notices a lamp fail along a route, or watches the stalker cross a distant opening. They can continue the objective, leave, lock a door, hide, reroute, or spend a resource.

The warning must belong to the actual approach. A global sting followed by contact from another direction is decoration, not information. Minimum warning time must be measured under supported captions, input, camera, and network conditions.

Not every warning needs to end in attack. Sometimes the player counteracts the route, the stalker passes elsewhere, or constraints invalidate the opportunity. This keeps signs informative rather than reducing them to mandatory pre-hit alarms.

## Claims are not behavior

Characters, notes, and local folklore can teach hypotheses about territory or behavior. They should remain attributed claims. A sincere witness may describe an old boundary that an objective later disables. A frightened resident may mistake correlation for a rule. The stalker follows authoritative world state; the evidence record preserves whether observation confirms or contradicts the claim.

This prevents two opposite failures: exposition that is always an answer key, and arbitrary betrayal of every stated rule. The player can use a claim provisionally, test it from a protected position, and update a route without the text being deleted or rewritten.

## Recovery restores the value of warning

After contact or successful counterplay, a bounded search and withdrawal let the immediate problem close. Presentation intensity falls, the director spends its budget, and another full episode becomes ineligible for a meaningful interval.

Without recovery, warning becomes continuous background noise and players remain in one defensive mode. With excessive scheduled calm, the threat becomes a predictable clock. Recovery should combine time with world state: the stalker must actually leave contact, and the player must not be punished for pausing, reading, or using a refuge.

Release and cooldown are different. Release clears confirmation through sensory loss or counterplay, completes the bounded search, and moves the stalker through a declared withdrawal connector. Cooldown controls when a later episode may be scheduled. Starting cooldown while the stalker still waits outside a hiding place creates a false end and allows overlapping pressure episodes.

The player needs an end cue available in supported modes: a visible crossing away, a ward returning, a directional caption and world motion, or another route-grounded signal. Silence alone can mean withdrawal, waiting, a muted device, or missing audio.

## Capture recovery should preserve learning, not confusion

A retry may retain discovered route knowledge, objective progress, or a recorded territory cue while resetting immediate pursuit. It may also retain a declared injury or spent resource when the campaign calls for it. The contract must name the result. Hidden pressure debt, a duplicated stalker, changed connector access, or a harsher repeated encounter with no explanation turns capture into technical uncertainty rather than learning.

## Counterplay needs to alter simulation state

A lock changes connectors. A distraction changes evidence. A hiding action breaks confirmed perception under declared conditions. A limited defense changes ability or distance. A route choice changes which zone can be reached first.

These actions matter because they alter the stalker's options, not because an animation says they worked. Each needs a cost or boundary so no single action becomes a permanent disable button. Encounter variety can then emerge from combining stable rules rather than scripting unrelated scares.

## Common counterexamples

### “Spawn behind the player because they cannot see it”

This ignores recent inspection, route continuity, audio, other players, mirrors, and doorway traps. Off-camera is only one candidate constraint.

### “Increase intensity until an encounter must happen”

If no legal site exists, forcing one violates the rules that make encounters fair. Budget should wait or produce non-contact evidence.

### “The director knows the player, so the stalker does too”

Scheduling information is not character knowledge. Passing the player's live transform into search makes every sensory and route rule cosmetic.

### “Teleport while the screen is black”

A hidden cut does not establish world continuity. Abstract travel still needs a route, elapsed time, and a legal materialization point unless teleportation is an explicit ability.

### “More encounters create more pressure”

Continuous contact often reduces tension and exhausts the mechanic. Variation in evidence, distance, objective exposure, counterplay, and recovery creates a more durable curve.

### “The room has the stalker's texture, so the player knows its territory”

Decoration does not establish reachability, knowledge, or behavior. Give the cue a connector, an observed crossing, or a changed world rule the player can test.

### “Search ends when the timer expires”

A timer may bound search breadth, but release still needs a valid loss of confirmation, completed search transition, and withdrawal route. Otherwise the stalker can stop while visibly holding current evidence.

## What a successful route lets the player explain

The player should be able to say, “The ash on the west latch meant it had crossed that bridge; restoring the vane signaled my wing; I locked the short connector, heard it take the gallery, and used the cooldown to reach the lift.” That explanation contains topology, evidence, consequence, counterplay, and recovery—the ingredients of systemic roaming pressure.
