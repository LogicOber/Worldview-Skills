# Worldview Game — Horror Role and Identity Pressure

Build a fair horror decision around who someone is, what role they claim, what permissions they legitimately hold, what each observer believes, and what happens if the player trusts or accuses the wrong person. Identity is tested through behavior, access, history, and evidence—not likeness or real-world traits.

## Call this Skill
```text
/worldview-game-horror-role-and-identity-pressure

Design a first-person scene at a storm shelter. A person wearing the relief
warden's coat can open the medicine cage but asks to move residents through a
door the real warden promised never to use. Separate body, presented identity,
role permissions, private knowledge, observer belief, and culpability. Give
question, comply, refuse, verify, and seek-witness paths with one ending.
```

Use it when the core question is recognition, authorization, trust, replacement, social expectation, or misuse of a legitimate role. Use perception distortion when the main decision is whether a sensory cue is reliable, and evidence identification when the main work is testing competing causal hypotheses.

## What the Agent delivers
- an identity-layer and permission matrix;
- schedules, access history, promises, private knowledge, and observer beliefs;
- fair verification actions and contaminated cues;
- trust, question, comply, refuse, report, and witness choices with consequences;
- false-positive recovery and accusation costs;
- save/load, viewpoint, accessibility, privacy, failure, and canonical-ending verification;
- runtime implementation when a project is available.
