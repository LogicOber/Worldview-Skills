# Original example — The Wrong Attendant

This fictional example demonstrates identity layers and permission-based trust. It is not an implemented game.

## Invocation
```text
/worldview-game-horror-role-and-identity-pressure

At a storm shelter, make the relief warden's identity uncertain without using
face-only guessing. The person in the coat can open the medicine cage but asks
to move residents through a prohibited lower door. Use access history, a private
play-derived fact, a witness, accusation recovery, and one canonical ending.
```

## Identity layers
`ACTOR-WARDEN-ONA` is the authoritative warden. `FORM-COAT-01` is clothing and badge, not identity. `ROLE-WARDEN` legitimately opens the medicine cage and assigns bunks but cannot authorize the lower flood door alone. Observer beliefs are stored per resident. Culpability is determined by actions and evidence.

The uncertain attendant presents Ona’s coat, voice, badge, and cage key. The actor ID remains unknown until tested. The fiction permits copied appearance and recent surface memory, but not knowledge learned before copying or the ability to cross a chalked drainage seam without disturbing it.

## Established history
During ordinary intake, Ona gives the player a cracked blue cup for resident Pell and promises never to use the lower door because its stairs flood before the gauge alarms. The player personally places the cup beside bunk 6. Volunteer Iri witnesses the promise from the registry table. Ona later opens the medicine cage correctly and logs two packs.

## Conflict
The returned attendant opens the cage with the correct key, proving access but not identity. They request moving residents through the lower door and claim Pell’s cup was green. A flickering lamp makes the face intermittently unfamiliar; this presentation cue is contaminated and not decisive.

## Tests
1. Ask where Pell’s cup was placed. Only a player who performed intake can evaluate “bunk 6, cracked blue.”
2. Ask the attendant to cross the drainage seam. The real Ona’s prior crossing left a known double heel mark; the copy disturbs chalk without that pattern.
3. Seek Iri, who can confirm the promise only if the player did not send Iri to the roof inventory.

## Choices and consequences
| Choice | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Comply | Lower route opens and saves time, but water isolates two bunks. The attendant gains access to the pump room. Evidence remains through chalk and cup. |
| Question | Costs storm time and reduces calm, but exposes the cup discrepancy. |
| Refuse | Preserves residents but closes medicine service until authority is re-established. |
| Verify seam | Produces physical evidence while exposing the player near the lower door. |
| Seek Iri | Adds witness corroboration if Iri is present; otherwise records last-known task rather than magically summoning them. |

## False accusation recovery
If the player accuses the real Ona in an earlier rehearsal, medicine access pauses and trust falls. The cage log, cup, and Iri’s testimony remain. Ona offers a bounded recheck using the seam and prior promise. Recovery costs time and one resident’s comfort pack but does not erase the ability to identify the later copy.

## Canonical ending
All histories converge when the lower gauge fails. The player uses the legitimate two-person authorization learned during intake: Ona or Iri turns the registry key while the player closes the lower valve. If the player trusted the copy, they must recover the registry key from the pump route; refusal preserves it at the desk. One ending evacuates residents to the roof, while trust, injury, and evidence aftermath differ locally.

## Required proof
- coat, body, actor, role, permissions, beliefs, and culpability remain separate;
- cage key access does not prove innocence;
- private-knowledge test is available only if learned;
- witness knowledge depends on presence/task;
- face flicker never becomes the sole cue;
- mistaken trust and false accusation remain recoverable;
- save/load reconstructs actor, permissions, chalk, cup, beliefs, and evidence;
- nonvisual, subtitle, non-color, reduced-intensity, and privacy-safe paths preserve judgment.
