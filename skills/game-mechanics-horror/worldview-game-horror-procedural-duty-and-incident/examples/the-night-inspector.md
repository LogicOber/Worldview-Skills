# Original example — The Night Inspector

This is an original fictional contract example, not an implemented or externally derived scene.

## Invocation
```text
/worldview-game-horror-procedural-duty-and-incident

Design a single-ending chapter about a night flood inspector checking three
river gates before a storm. A physical gauge should disagree with the remote
log while a downstream warning remains due. Let procedure, improvisation,
retreat/report, and task transfer preserve different evidence and consequences.
```

## Duty and authority
Inspector Mara may unlock gauge cages, seal a paper reading, stop one gate for inspection, and issue a temporary downstream warning. She must record three gates before 02:00. She may not alter historical logs or run two gates in manual mode. Dispatcher Sen may acknowledge warnings and operate one gate remotely but cannot touch physical seals.

Normal Gate A teaches the river path, manual wheel, dry witness stamp, and downstream call box. Gate A’s signed reading later proves how legitimate pressure and seal depth look. The procedure therefore owns route rehearsal, an affordance, and evidence.

## Live work before the incident
At Gate B, the player has committed water height but not seal condition. The downstream warning is due in an estimated 210 seconds. Gate C remains unread. The gate controller continues its scheduled pulse while the cage is open. Progress at “height committed” survives interruption; the unstable wheel rotation reverts to its last notch.

## Incident
The remote log claims Gate B is closed and dry. The physical gauge shows rising water, the chain is wet, and the cage seal appears intact. The player can compare the Gate A stamp, inspect chain direction, call Sen, or open the service slot. A second explanation remains possible: a downstream pressure reversal rather than a false log.

Unseen in the office above, groundskeeper Iven can observe the cage through a grated floor only while his lamp is on. The grate casts a visible stripe and the player hears one chair movement, making witness possibility fair.

## Response matrix
| Response | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Procedure | Seal the discrepancy, call Sen, wait for acknowledgement. Preserves custody but spends 95 seconds and lets water close the short route to Gate C. |
| Improvise | Use the service slot to attach a ribbon to the moving chain. Reveals flow direction quickly but breaks the untouched-seal claim and gives Iven grounds to report tampering. |
| Retreat/report | Issue the downstream warning and leave the cage. Protects residents and transmits knowledge, but Gate B evidence remains incomplete and the main office later questions the alarm. |
| Transfer | Assign Sen the remote pulse while Mara inspects the chain. If communication drops, the controller returns to scheduled mode and records Sen’s last confirmed step. |

All paths reach the same flood-control ending. They change route, credibility, evidence custody, and whether Iven later assists.

## Responsibility shift
After the discrepancy, “correct inspection” no longer means completing three readings. It means proving which control channel can physically move water before the storm pulse. The player uses the Gate A manual-wheel lesson at Gate C, where the remote log requests an impossible close. A physical counterweight position—rather than the displayed status—reveals the safe sequence.

## Failure and recovery
The likely error is opening Gate B fully to test it. Water enters the service path, but a warning bell and rising-line marker provide six estimated seconds to retreat. Failure reloads at the call box with committed Gate A evidence, the Gate B discrepancy, response choice, and remaining deadline intact. The route is longer, not unknowable.

## Canonical ending and audit
Mara manually uncouples Gate C from the false control pulse and uses the warning line to signal downstream evacuation. The ending audit lists Gate A complete, Gate B disputed with response-specific custody, Gate C uncoupled, Gate B/C remote logs quarantined, and the warning delivered. Iven’s testimony exists only if his sightline was active. The dawn office cannot return to ordinary operation because physical and remote records now visibly disagree.

## Required proof
- normal procedure produces authoritative evidence;
- deadline continues during incident according to contract;
- partial work and transfer survive save/load;
- witness knowledge depends on sightline and attention;
- every response changes local state and still reaches the ending;
- manual-wheel final action was taught safely;
- muted audio, non-color gauge, subtitles, assisted timing, and reduced intensity work;
- all estimated margins are measured in runtime.
