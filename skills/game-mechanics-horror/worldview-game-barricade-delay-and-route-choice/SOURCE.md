## Original design question

The method begins from one question: when does moving an object in front of a door become a meaningful player decision rather than a slow animation?

The answer developed here is that the barrier must exchange one spatial future for another. It purchases a measurable interval, but placement consumes something, another route changes, and pressure eventually returns. That causal contract generated the state model, useful-delay calculation, reachability checks, persistence rules, and direct verification plan.

## Authored decisions

The following are original editorial and design decisions in this Skill:

- defining useful time after placement and travel costs rather than treating durability as the result;
- requiring the before, blocked, breached, and restored route graphs to agree across collision and navigation;
- treating route loss or future access as part of the price;
- separating visual physics settling from authoritative sealing;
- requiring a readable breach phase and stable threat commitment;
- testing irreversible placement for softlocks from both sides;
- packaging a contract, tunables, direct evidence, and an original fictional example.

These decisions are proposals for a reusable method. They are not empirical claims about player preference or universal balance.

## Original example
