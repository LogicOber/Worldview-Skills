# Why relief resources with hidden costs fail

## One meter erases the decision

If use lowers “fear” now but the same meter later becomes “corruption,” the player cannot tell whether relief worked or the interface renamed a punishment. Store relief and cost independently. Let each affect concrete actions through its owner.

## Rational first use becomes an author trap

A player who uses the only offered aid under severe pressure has not made a meaningful mistake if the fatal cost had no cue. A first surprise can establish uncertainty. Repeated or irreversible harm requires learnable evidence, an alternative, and a chance to act on the rule.

## The alternative is fictional

“Refuse” is not an alternative if it immediately returns to the same prompt or guarantees failure. Give refusal a playable cost: slower movement, a longer safe route, help debt, lost privacy, or additional manual work. Verify the no-use route to the ending.

## Relief is fake

Some designs play a soothing effect but preserve the same control penalty. This trains distrust without a tradeoff. An accepted use must produce the declared immediate improvement and expose its duration.

## Cost appears only at the ending

An ending reveal cannot support midgame decisions. Show the first trace early enough to test, provide a repeated confirmation before the risky threshold, and disclose the coupling before irreversible commitment.

## Inventory count becomes bodily or narrative truth

Having three doses does not mean the character used three, and using three does not tell a body, institution, or threat how to react. Inventory owns availability and transfer. The relief ledger owns accepted use. Receiving systems own resulting condition, permission, or alert state.

## The comfort item dominates every preparation

If relief is large, cost is delayed, and the alternative is weak, carrying the maximum is automatic. Balance with forecastable access, context-specific value, thresholds, and viable coping—not arbitrary weight inflation or a hidden damage multiplier.

## Compulsion is framed as player stupidity

Repeated use often occurs because pressure, route, or feedback makes it rational. Record what the player knew at each use. If the game wants escalating concern, change the evidence and available alternatives before judging the decision.

## Exhaustion creates a dead save

Supply depletion should reveal the fallback, not end the campaign by omission. Separate “none available” from refusal and ensure every supported history retains one route to the fixed ending.

## Accessibility reveals too much or too little

Reduced intensity must still communicate cost. Assistance must not disclose an undiscovered rule prematurely. Use equivalent shape, label, vibration, caption, and timing cues tied to the same knowledge stage.

## Boundary test

Use scarce-inventory triage when the decision is what to carry. Use wounds, infection, and treatment when the decision is physical assessment and treatment. Use this Skill when the implementation must couple real immediate relief to a separate delayed consequence without letting either neighboring system overwrite the other.
