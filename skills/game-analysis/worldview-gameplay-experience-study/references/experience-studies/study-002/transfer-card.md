# Transfer card — terrain makes the objective alter the escape graph

`study_id: study-002`
`tags: infrastructure, water, slow movement, timed escape`
`confidence: medium`

## Keep the relationship

An objective should change the terrain or route timing so that progress also changes what movement and resource rules mean.

## Replace the surface

- setting and architecture: a tide gate beneath a transit station
- player role and objective: inspection worker who must equalize pressure and reach a dry service stair
- threat or opposing system: rising water plus a timed obstruction system
- visual language: concrete, hazard paint, reflected work lights, and visible depth markers
- sound language: pump cycles, pressure knocks, water level change, and doors timing out
- object/resource language: valve handle, battery, dry-route marker, temporary door release

## Build sequence

1. **Setup the expectation:** reveal the valve, the dry shortcut, and the flooded alternative from one approach.
2. **Place the readable affordance:** make the pressure gauge and next obstruction visible during the interaction.
3. **Introduce the uncertainty or cost:** the valve changes water level and removes sprint reliability.
4. **Let the player choose:** spend battery on a fast route or accept a slower route with more cover.
5. **Deliver the consequence:** doors buy seconds in sequence, while water makes each missed decision costly.
6. **Allow the player to explain the outcome:** the gauge and obstruction timing show why the run failed.
7. **Carry the learned rule forward:** a later section changes the terrain rule again instead of applying a constant slow debuff.

## Failure boundary

Do not slow every corridor equally or hide the next obstruction; otherwise the mechanic is fatigue, not route strategy.

## First-time-player test

- Before the beat, what does the tester predict?
- What do they look at, listen for, or remember?
- What choice do they make without a tutorial prompt?
- After a failure, can they explain what they will change?
- After success, can they explain why the route worked?
