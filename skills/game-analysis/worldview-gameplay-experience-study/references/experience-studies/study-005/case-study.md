# Study 005 — reading and moving compete for the same attention

`tags:` attention budget, notes, vertical risk, bright/dark contrast, partial read

## Player voice

I want to read the report, but the room is still physically vulnerable. I take the actionable phrase and leave the rest because I cannot keep the page, the doorway, the sound, and the route in focus at once. A floor break later removes the horizon, so I use a wall or material change to regain control.

When the corridor becomes bright, I do not feel automatically safe. I feel visible. The route asks me to time a crossing instead of hiding inside darkness.

## Critic voice

This is good design when the player must prioritize information rather than complete a document checklist. The vertical transition is a useful stress test of attention, but it becomes unfair if the landing has no anchor. Brightness is a meaningful escalation only when it removes concealment.

## Transfer

Use a hospital records room with a note visible beside a glass corridor. Partial reading should reveal a timing rule; a stair landing should reveal a colored pipe or audible machine; the bright corridor should contain one visible crossing and one delayed cover. Test partial-read comprehension and landing recovery.

`quality:` effective when the player can explain what they chose not to attend to; weak when every detail is mandatory.
