# Study 006 — late clarity can make the player less safe

`tags:` surveillance, visual reliability, bright exposure, finale compression, system space

## Player voice

The early broken floor and dark routes teach me to search for physical anchors. Later the facility becomes bright, clinical, and industrial. I can see the rooms, screens, glass, and catwalks, but that clarity makes it harder to disappear. A monitor may show a useful preview, a delayed state, or a lie; I compare it with the door and floor instead of trusting it completely.

When the image distorts, I return to the wall, railing, or sound direction. By the final route I do not want another maze. I want a short push where the rules I learned finally matter and the last state change is visible.

## Critic voice

The late escalation succeeds because it changes the information regime. Fear moves from “I cannot see” to “I can see, but I cannot trust what the system means.” The finale is strongest when the topology compresses and consequence increases. Distortion without a physical anchor is merely frustrating spectacle.

## Transfer

Use a floodlit offshore station with radar screens, glass, wet steel, and a catwalk. Let one monitor preview a state that the physical route corrects. Keep the final graph short; use timing and exposure instead of a new maze. Test whether the player can name the monitor prediction, the correction cue, and the final route.

`quality:` memorable when visibility changes responsibility; unfair when every information channel fails at once.
