# Study 004 — visibility removes concealment

`tags:` outdoor exposure, light, recurring figure, landmark loop, endurance

## Player voice

The outdoor space is brighter than the corridors, but rain, glare, and distance make every highlight uncertain. I move toward a shed because it is smaller and has edges; inside, a mark on the floor makes the door feel like a decision rather than a refuge. A pale figure reappears near a fence, and I am afraid before I know whether it moves.

The fountain or bright tank becomes the coordinate I can trust. The long path is tiring when it supplies no new landmark, but the material change into a tiled service room lets me stop scanning bushes and start looking for a system.

## Critic voice

The strong relationship is “more visible, less hidden.” The scene is not frightening because the player cannot see; it is frightening because the player can see how exposed the crossing is. The weak stretch is an unmarked search that asks the player to keep walking without changing the question.

## Transfer

Use a floodlit weather yard with one recognizable pump tower and a small maintenance shelter. Let the shelter be a temporary decision box, not a safe room. Test whether players can return to the pump tower and explain what changed there.

`quality:` memorable when the landmark closes a loop; merely atmospheric when the light has no route consequence.
