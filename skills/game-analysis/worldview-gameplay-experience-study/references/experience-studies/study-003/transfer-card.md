# Transfer card — partial reading creates a testable spatial hypothesis

`study_id: study-003`
`tags: documents, threshold memory, vertical transition, landmarks`
`confidence: medium`

## Keep the relationship

Information is valuable when the player can read only a usable fragment, carry a hypothesis through a physical threshold, and test it in space.

## Replace the surface

- setting and architecture: an archive with a live sightline, service stairs, and a valve room
- player role and objective: records technician tracing a pressure fault
- threat or opposing system: exposure during reading and unreliable horizon after a vertical move
- visual language: torn maintenance tags, colored pipes, and one distinctive landing material
- sound language: overhead hum, pressure release, and a directionally stable machine tone
- object/resource language: handheld note, valve diagram fragment, flashlight, stair rail

## Build sequence

1. **Setup the expectation:** place the note beside a doorway that remains visible.
2. **Place the readable affordance:** highlight one phrase or symbol that can be remembered without a full read.
3. **Introduce the uncertainty or cost:** reading freezes movement and narrows monitoring.
4. **Let the player choose:** finish the note, take the fragment, or leave and test a guess.
5. **Deliver the consequence:** a stair or drop removes the horizon, then a material and sound anchor restores orientation.
6. **Allow the player to explain the outcome:** the next valve relationship confirms, reverses, or complicates the phrase.
7. **Carry the learned rule forward:** later notes remain hypotheses, not collectible answers.

## Failure boundary

A document is noise if no later space responds to it. A vertical transition is unfair if the landing supplies no recoverable shape, light, or sound.

## First-time-player test

- Before the beat, what does the tester predict?
- What do they look at, listen for, or remember?
- What choice do they make without a tutorial prompt?
- After a failure, can they explain what they will change?
- After success, can they explain why the route worked?
