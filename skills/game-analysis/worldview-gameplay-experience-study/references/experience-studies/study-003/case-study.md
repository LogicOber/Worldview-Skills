# Study 003 — a note spends mobility to create a hypothesis

`tags:` documents, threshold memory, vertical transition, partial information, landmark

## Player voice

I stop to read while a doorway remains in the edge of my vision. The note gives me an idea about the place, but reading freezes my movement and narrows what I can monitor. I do not need every sentence; I need one phrase that I can test against the next room.

When the route drops or climbs, the camera removes my horizon. A structural landmark after the landing is more useful than another frightening prop because it tells me how to rebuild the map.

## Critic voice

The note is successful only if a later physical space confirms, reverses, or complicates the hypothesis. Otherwise it is lore noise. The vertical transition is fair when the loss of control is followed by a recoverable shape, light, or sound. A blind landing is not automatically suspense.

## Transfer

Place a torn maintenance tag beside a live sightline in an archive. Let partial reading identify a valve relationship, then test it in the next room. Use a unique floor material or overhead hum after a vertical move. Test whether players can state the hypothesis without reading every line.

`quality:` memorable when information changes route behavior; forgettable when the text never returns.
