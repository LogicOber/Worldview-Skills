# Study 001 — the player learns to wait for a second confirmation

`tags:` camera cost, sound prediction, hiding, route learning, recovery

## Player voice

I enter a dark facility with a camera that is also my only reliable way to see. The battery counter turns every doorway into a decision. After I operate a machine, I hear activity but cannot place the threat. I hide in a cabinet because I saw it before the danger started; I am not being handed a magic safe room.

The nearby cabinet opens. Mine does not. I leave with a rule that feels useful but not guaranteed: perhaps the search depends on being seen. Later I leave after the footsteps fade and discover that quiet was not enough. The next time I wait, I am listening for a second confirmation. When an impact commits the threat to a door, I use that animation as a short escape window instead of running blindly.

## Critic voice

This works because sound is attached to a decision, not a mood layer. The player is allowed to be almost right, then correct the belief. The strongest result is not “the monster was scary”; it is “my next attempt changed because I understood why the previous attempt failed.”

The weak point is spatial recovery after the successful objective: if the player completes the switch but cannot find the return route, confusion can feel like missing landmark support rather than deliberate mystery. The original abstract plate keeps the relationship—question → cover → cue → false branch → recovery—and replaces every surface.

## Transfer

Use a hand-crank inspection lamp, a pressure valve, shutters, and a new search system in a flooded archive. Show the shutter before the threat; let a mixed sound cue create a false-safe interval; give the player a marked return point after the valve. Test whether first-time players can explain why they waited and why the first exit failed.

`quality:` memorable and fair when the cue is readable; unfair when the audio is indistinguishable or the cover appears only after danger begins.
