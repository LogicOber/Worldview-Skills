# Transfer card — delayed confirmation turns hiding into route learning

`study_id: study-001`
`tags: sound prediction, scarce visibility, cover, recovery`
`confidence: medium`

## Keep the relationship

A scarce visibility tool and a readable sound sequence should make the player wait, test a belief, and change the next escape attempt.

## Replace the surface

- setting and architecture: a flooded records annex with shutters, valves, and marked return points
- player role and objective: night maintenance worker who must restore pressure and leave
- threat or opposing system: a search process that reacts to exposure and door state
- visual language: wet metal, hand-painted maintenance marks, narrow pools of lamp light
- sound language: motor hum, displaced water, impact confirmation, and silence that is never sufficient by itself
- object/resource language: hand-crank lamp, pressure gauge, shutter latch

## Build sequence

1. **Setup the expectation:** show the shutter and lamp before the valve interaction.
2. **Place the readable affordance:** let the lamp reveal the valve and the marked return route.
3. **Introduce the uncertainty or cost:** turning the valve fixes the player's view while a mixed sound cue begins a search.
4. **Let the player choose:** hide, leave immediately, or wait for a second confirmation.
5. **Deliver the consequence:** an adjacent door opens first; a later impact commits the search to the player's door.
6. **Allow the player to explain the outcome:** the impact and the visible shutter make the escape window legible.
7. **Carry the learned rule forward:** a later room tests whether the player waits for commitment rather than quiet alone.

## Failure boundary

The relationship becomes unfair when the sound layers cannot be separated, the cover appears only after the threat starts, or the return route has no landmark.

## First-time-player test

- Before the beat, what does the tester predict?
- What do they look at, listen for, or remember?
- What choice do they make without a tutorial prompt?
- After a failure, can they explain what they will change?
- After success, can they explain why the route worked?
