# Quality judgment guide

Use a judgment only with a reason tied to a player expectation, a concrete cue, and a consequence. These words are not a universal score.

| Judgment | Use it when | Required reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| effective | The beat produces a deliberate, understandable player decision. | Name the cue, the decision, and the resulting change in belief. |
| memorable | The moment remains in attention after it ends because of a specific relationship or image. | Name what the player can later recall and why. |
| fair | A first-time player can understand success or failure after the feedback. | Name the teachable cue and the recovery or retry path. |
| weak | The intended pressure exists but the player decision is thin or repetitive. | Say what the player does instead of deciding. |
| unfair | The game demands an unseen fact, impossible timing, or unprovided memory. | Name the missing cue or unreadable failure. |
| forgettable | The beat is technically competent but leaves no changed belief, route, or relationship. | Name what failed to persist in attention. |
| funny | Humor changes the player's attention or releases pressure without deleting the stakes. | Name the setup and the timing of the release. |
| beautiful | Composition, movement, light, sound, or material makes the player stop and look for a reason. | Name what the player notices and whether it affects play. |
| merely loud | Volume, motion, or a sudden reveal creates arousal without a meaningful choice. | Name the missing decision or after-effect. |
| unresolved | The footage or edit cannot support a quality conclusion. | Name the missing view, retry, audio stem, or context. |

Never use “immersive,” “cinematic,” “good pacing,” or “scary” as the whole judgment. Those words may appear only after the concrete reason.
