# Example: The Last Service Floor

This is an original fictional example of a small horror production contract. It demonstrates the reasoning shape; its title, setting, values, and names are not defaults.

## Premise

An overnight maintenance worker must restart the pumps beneath a closed public aquarium. The building is not haunted in every room. Instead, each restart changes which part of the building is willing to remember the worker.

## Player promise

The player fears being observed by a presence that can use reflected water and delayed sound, but can survive by reading the building's service markings, choosing when to make noise, and retreating before a route becomes irreversible.

## Chapter rhythm

| Beat | Player experience | Design function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orientation | Find the first pump room and learn the yellow maintenance stripe. | Establish a reliable spatial anchor. |
| Invitation | A dead intercom repeats the player's last tool sound from another corridor. | Teach delayed sound without revealing the threat. |
| Confirmation | The same corridor returns with one tank light on and a wet handprint above the stripe. | Make return state meaningful. |
| Complication | Restarting the pump drains a safe stair and opens a shorter service route. | Turn progress into route cost. |
| Pressure | A pursuit begins after a deliberate noise choice; the main route requires two visible commitments and the alternative costs the only dry fuse. | Make the chase a learned building problem. |
| Recovery | A dry filtration room lets the player map the next sound and hear the threat behind a wall. | Restore agency without erasing state. |
| Ending | The first yellow stripe is seen from inside the empty tank; the player understands the building has been tracking maintenance visits, not hunting randomly. | Reframe the opening anchor. |

## Transferable design rule

The example does not require a water monster, an aquarium, or a maintenance job. The transferable rule is: establish one reliable spatial cue, let a system replay a player action with a delay, change a returning space after progress, and make the late route test the player's accumulated knowledge rather than a new button sequence.
