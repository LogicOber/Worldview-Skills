# Worked example — The Lantern Index

This is an original fictional worked example for `worldview-game-single-ending-campaign`. It demonstrates how one canonical ending can support a dense campaign rather than a straight corridor or a collection of errands.

No game, source code, model, audio file, deployed page, benchmark, or playtest was produced for this document. Every technical value is an implementation proposal. All names, institutions, sayings, events, mechanics, and visual designs below were created for this example. They do not depict a real person, company, product, dispute, logo, interface, or allegation.

## Starting request

> Make an English browser 3D satirical horror game about a city that lets one office decide which lamps deserve to shine. The player should expose the system by using light, not weapons. Give me one ending, several kinds of play, a world that changes, and about half an hour of play.

## Lock 3 — Canonical ending

### Ending invariant

> At the top of the Dawn Spire, Mara installs the copied calibration grammar in the city’s dawn organ and turns its three playable rings into agreement. Every surviving public lamp then gains the ability to teach its light pattern to another lamp. The central Index remains physically present but loses exclusive control of calibration. Mara watches one child teach a dark lamp to shine as the title returns. This is the only completed campaign outcome.

| Field | Locked value |
| --- | --- |
| Final playable action | Rotate three rings using the same reveal, share, and conceal rules learned earlier while the Audit Choir closes in. |
| Irreversible commit | `campaign.endingCommitted` changes from `false` to `true` after all rings agree and Mara presses the lit plate into the organ. |
| Required knowledge | The displayed brightness score measures registered lineage; the hidden calibration grammar can be carried by copied light. |
| Required mastery | Beam tuning, wick allocation, light sharing, threat concealment, and mirror traversal. |
| World result | Public lamps begin peer teaching; the Index can observe but no longer grant or revoke the only valid pattern. |
| Emotional payoff | The city changes through a learned, repeated player verb rather than a speech or a binary menu. |
| Control boundary | Player retains camera control during the propagation wave; locomotion ends only when Mara reaches the roof rail and the child relights the street lamp. |
| Post-ending | One fixed 18-second playable-look aftermath, then credits and `Restart campaign`. No relationship montage changes the outcome. |

Route history changes the finale’s pressure, not its ending:

| Prior route | Journey and finale difference | Ending invariant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quota Works | Mara carries a copper bypass; one ring turns cheaply, but Warden Noell arrives early because the works route stamped Mara’s lamp. Tern physically holds one shutter if previously helped. | Same organ commit, city propagation, roof image, and meaning. |
| Whisper Reservoir | Mara carries a cold-glass echo; hidden auditors are visible, but each ring consumes more wick. Sella calls the safe harmonic interval if previously helped. | Same organ commit, city propagation, roof image, and meaning. |

### Final ontology and correction chain

`final_ontology`: the Index does not measure inherent brightness. Its failed sensor recognizes registered lineage, and copied light can carry the hidden calibration grammar without central permission.

| Unresolved question | Original event | Earlier interpretation | Corrective evidence | Revised interpretation | Changed action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Why do bright public lamps fail certification? | restored market lamps reject copied patterns | storm damage makes the Index miscount brightness | route plates show meters assigned before testing; reservoir patterns remain stable outside registration | certification measures lineage rather than light quality | stop repairing brightness and build a carrier for copied grammar |
| Why can the Office revoke every lamp? | one central calibration wave disables unregistered patterns | the center contains a uniquely necessary signal | Peer Vault shelves teach identical patterns laterally when one lamp shares with the next | the center monopolizes distribution, not capability | install the copied grammar in the dawn organ and make each ring teach another |

### Finale teaching and learned containment

| Final action or rule | Prior tutorial beat | Pressured rehearsal | Finale use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tune reveal / bind / mute bands | three quiet market lamps accept one band each | Meter Court floor changes while Noell searches | align each organ ring under pursuit |
| share one wick and copied pattern | Mara relights Tern's shelter lamp and sees it relay to a second lamp | Peer Vault shelves must pass a pattern before Noell extinguishes the source | each ring teaches the next instead of asking the Index |
| contain a listener with a closed calibration path | a harmless auditor loops between two test lamps in Tallow Market until Mara breaks the path | a Peer Vault shelf loop delays Noell but spends one wick | route-specific carrier closes a temporary decoy loop around the Audit Choir, creating time for the same organ commit |

The containment path is not a new finale genre. The player learned its landmark, cost, release rule, and recovery earlier. Ignoring it leaves a harder but viable ring sequence; using it changes pursuit pressure, not the ending.

### Local humane decision

In Act I, Mara may spend one scarce wick to relight Tern's public shelter before printing a route plate. This is locally costly and never required for the ending. If done, Tern later holds a Spire shutter long enough to widen one recovery window, and the aftermath shows the shelter teaching light to its neighboring stairwell. If skipped, Mara uses a taught manual shutter crank and the shelter remains dark in the fixed roof view. Both histories commit the same grammar, disable exclusive calibration, and reach the same title return.

### Mandatory aftermath state

- `endingCommitted=true` and `indexExclusiveCalibration=false` persist.
- The Index remains physically present, so the ending does not pretend the institution vanished.
- Public lamps retain peer-teaching state after reload.
- Route carrier, remaining wick, Tern shelter state, and surviving evidence remain as local aftermath variation.
- Reloading the post-ending record returns to the roof look state and cannot recommit the organ transaction.
- Starting a new campaign requires the explicit new-run flow and clears campaign state without changing settings.

Capture, extinguishment, falling, quitting, declining an optional conversation, and allowing an NPC to leave are failure or state variations, never endings.

## Lock 5 — Gameplay portfolio

### Six reusable systems

| ID | Player verb | Information before commitment | Cost / risk | State and feedback | Campaign growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `G1 Tune` | Rotate the lamp between reveal, bind, and mute bands. | Materials shimmer with one of three edge rhythms; captions name the rhythm. | Wrong band can erase a temporary trace or alert a listener. | Target state changes only after 0.6 s stable aim; beam shape, hand pose, tone, and reticle agree. | Starts with static markings, grows into moving mirrors, NPC-held surfaces, and three-ring alignment. |
| `G2 Allocate` | Move finite wick units among personal lamp, world lamp, plate, and mechanism. | Exact current/required units and refund rule are visible. | More personal brightness improves reading but increases threat range; committed copies are not always refundable. | One resource owner publishes every change to lamp, UI, NPC, and threat systems. | Later encounters require investing now to create a future safe route. |
| `G3 Compose` | Arrange observed proof glyphs into a three-part audit sentence. | Each glyph shows source location and literal effect; no trivia guess. | Only one active sentence; changing it consumes time under pressure. | The environment executes subject–measure–exception as a state rule. | Moves from opening a door to making a floor, patrol, or scale inspect itself. |
| `G4 Conceal` | Break the harmonic path between Mara’s lamp and an Audit Choir listener. | Cones appear through sound direction, ceramic head angle, and floor resonance. | Muting hides light but also removes reveal cues; stamped light can be tracked through walls. | Detection has suspicion, verify, pursue, and recover states with distinct cues. | Threats learn route-shaped searches; no global omniscience or instant failure. |
| `G5 Share` | Give an NPC or lamp one wick and a copied pattern. | Recipient intent, immediate action, and permanent cost are shown. | A shared unit leaves Mara’s current reserve. | Recipient changes schedule or performs one systemic action, not merely a line. | Early sharing opens local shortcuts; later allies can hold a finale mechanism. |
| `G6 Traverse` | Aim mirrors, ride counterweighted platforms, and cross folding light bridges. | Motion path and load state are visible before stepping on. | Holding a bridge consumes light; fast crossings increase detection rhythm. | Traversal reads the same wick and tuning state as puzzles and stealth. | Combines with pursuit and resource redistribution in the Spire. |

### Interlocks

| System | Enables | Pressures | Degenerate strategy prevented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tune | Reveals proof, binds mechanisms, mutes detection | Requires exposure time | Surfaces accept only their readable band; blind cycling resets stability. |
| Allocate | Powers shares, bridges, plates, and safe lamps | Bright reserve increases visibility; low reserve narrows information | Wick is conserved across owners and cannot be duplicated outside authored copy events. |
| Compose | Rewrites local system rules | Requires evidence collected through exploration | Glyphs are contextual verbs, not universal keys; sentences have one scope and one active slot. |
| Conceal | Makes risky tuning and traversal possible | Muting removes navigation information | Auditors reposition toward the last causal event rather than forgetting immediately. |
| Share | Changes NPC schedules and creates remote actions | Permanently moves a wick unit until a declared reclaim point | Every recipient has one authored action and may refuse when its agenda conflicts. |
| Traverse | Turns light state into spatial consequence | Exposes the player during commitment | Platforms and bridges use authoritative energy rather than animation-only timing. |

The portfolio produces investigation, spatial puzzle, resource strategy, social consequence, stealth, traversal, and pursuit from the same light state. It does not bolt on combat, crafting, collectibles, or dialogue trees merely to increase feature count.

## Lock 9 — Visual, audio, and asset representation

### Original presentation grammar

| Axis | Rule and gameplay purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Shape | The Office uses tall ceramic housings pinched at the center; resident repairs use low wide frames and visible knots. A readable waist line distinguishes controlled from shared infrastructure at distance. |
| Surface | Glazed ceramic, wet slate, soot linen, hammered brass, blackened wick, and translucent vellum respond differently to the same lamp. Wear follows hands, runoff, heat, feet, and maintenance openings. |
| Light | Registered light is narrow and vertical; shared light spreads laterally between surfaces. Danger is also communicated by resonance and silhouette so color is never the only cue. |
| Color | Storm blue and bone ceramic form the world; warm tallow shows navigable shelter; a thin plum line marks Audit Choir verification. Decorative lights never borrow system colors. |
| Motion | Shared lamps answer with a sideways pulse. Office meters ratchet downward even when physical brightness rises. Auditors turn their entire ceramic head before their body commits. |
| Camera | Third-person, 55° vertical FOV, 3.1 m nominal boom, 1.5 m target height, authored shoulder swaps in narrow aisles, collision rather than wall clipping, reduced-motion alternative to impulses. |
| Interface | Original narrow sans for operational text, large wick allocations beside their world owner, input prompts bound to current device, full captions with speaker and non-speech light cues. |
| Sound | Ceramic resonance communicates threat phase; wick crackle communicates allocation; lateral lamp answers form a learned interval; the final city wave uses the same interval at architectural scale. |

No asset is a near likeness, real logo, product screen, generated text texture, or unexplained downloaded pack. Repetition comes from authored modular families, not hundreds of unrelated primitives.
